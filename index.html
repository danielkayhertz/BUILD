The Solution

What the BUILD Act Does

Common-sense reforms to help more Illinoisans find a home they can afford. Not radical — reasonable.

🏘️

More Homes by Right

Allow 2–8 homes on residential lots currently zoned single-family, based on lot size:

  • 2,500–5,000 sq ft lot → up to 4 units
  • 5,000–7,500 sq ft lot → up to 6 units
  • 7,500+ sq ft lot → up to 8 units

Permits and building inspections still required. Local design standards preserved.

🏡

Backyard Cottages (ADUs)

Legalize accessory dwelling units on all residential properties statewide — in-law suites, garage apartments, and backyard cottages.

Creates naturally affordable rental options near existing jobs, transit, and services. Helps homeowners build equity while adding housing.

📋

Cut the Red Tape

Reduce unnecessary delays that make housing more expensive to build:

  • Statewide timelines for permit reviews
  • Third-party inspectors if deadlines are missed
  • No parking minimums for missing middle housing
  • Standardized impact fee practices
💰

$250 Million Investment

Targeted funding to make housing affordable, not just possible:

  • $100M — site infrastructure (sewer, utilities)
  • $100M — middle housing via IHDA
  • $50M — first-time homebuyer assistance

Builds on Opening Doors (12,000 served since 2020) and SmartBuy programs.

Common Questions

We’ve Heard the Concerns.

Here are the facts.

No. BUILD doesn’t rezone anything — it sets statewide minimums for what’s allowed by right. Communities still control design standards, setbacks, landscaping requirements, historic district protections, and floodplain rules. Local officials remain in charge of zoning classifications. What changes is the floor, not the ceiling.

Illinois already sets statewide minimums for building codes, fire safety, and accessibility. Housing is no different.

Most new housing created under BUILD will be small-scale — duplexes, triplexes, backyard cottages. These are naturally more affordable than single-family homes because they share land and infrastructure costs.

The $250M investment specifically targets affordable and workforce housing development through IHDA. First-time homebuyer programs have already served over 13,000 Illinoisans since 2020.

BUILD allows gradual infill — a few units here and there across neighborhoods — not large-scale development. The impact on any single school or utility district is modest.

The $100M in site infrastructure funding directly addresses utility capacity. Communities that already meet IHDA affordability thresholds are well-positioned to absorb modest growth.

Some are — but statewide, Illinois has been building 19,000 homes per year when it needs 45,000. The gap isn’t closing, and it’s been widening for years.

Voluntary, community-by-community approaches haven’t worked at scale. Statewide minimum standards are how we solve problems that cross municipal lines — the same way we handle clean water, fire safety, and accessible design.

BUILD uses a tiered lot-size system precisely to account for variation. Tiny lots get fewer units; larger lots can have more. Local communities retain design standards, historic district protections, and floodplain rules.

What BUILD does is prevent any community from entirely banning modest increases in housing supply — because when every suburb says no, the housing crisis is everyone’s problem.
